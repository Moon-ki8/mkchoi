This project examines how interface dislocations move, interact, and control frictional response in layered 2D materials.
The central question is how defect mechanics at the interface can be translated into measurable signatures of superlubricity.

The work links atomistic descriptions of dislocation motion with continuum-style interpretations of defect mobility and stability.
That connection makes it possible to compare microscopic mechanisms with effective mechanical behavior.

## Focus

- Mobility of interface dislocations in bilayer graphene
- Defect-controlled friction and superlubric response
- Continuum interpretations of atomistic interfacial mechanics
